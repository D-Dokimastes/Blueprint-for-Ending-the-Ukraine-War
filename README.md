# Blueprint-for-Ending-the-Ukraine-War

A framework for negotiating an end to the Russia–Ukraine war. It develops a possible settlement architecture covering armed neutrality, territorial reintegration, constitutional restructuring, Crimea, Sevastopol, sanctions, security guarantees and implementation. A starting point for negotiation, not a finished treaty. v18. CC0 1.0 Universal

One possible path from war toward a peace settlement that neither side would regard as ideal, but both might eventually regard as safer than continuation of war:

The Central Exchange:
Ukraine regains its unity on the basis of the 1991 land border and gains Swiss-style armed neutrality;
Russia gains an effective guarantee that Ukraine cannot become a platform against Russia (no NATO, no foreign combat bases), sees Russian-language regional life protected inside a federal Ukraine and receives sanctions relief wired into the treaty itself, triggered automatically as agreed milestones are met.

The Crimean Exchange: Ukraine gains a seat in Crimean sovereignty; Russia keeps its seat as co-principal. Exclusive control is traded for a shared-sovereignty, Andorra-style constitution and local self-government with protected Crimean-Tatar rights. Neither side can militarise or absorb the peninsula, but citizens of both have full access.

Sevastopol: a 25-year, verified fleet draw-down ends with Sevastopol as a permanently demilitarised Free Trade Zone— no Russian, Ukrainian, or third-state naval base thereafter. One city, one rule: Free Trade.

Peace becomes possible when both principals accept limits they would rather avoid, because the alternative has become more dangerous than the compromise.
